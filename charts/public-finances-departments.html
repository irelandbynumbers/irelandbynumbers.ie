    About this data

    These figures are voted spending, the money the Dáil approves for each government department and office each year, grouped into Votes. They are counted in cash, by department, and do not include spending by local authorities from their own income, interest on the national debt, or the Social Insurance Fund, which pays the State Pension (Contributory) and other social insurance benefits. They are measured differently from the general government figures in the charts above, so the totals do not match.

    Amounts are gross spending, before deducting appropriations-in-aid, which are receipts a department keeps, such as pension contributions from staff and EU funding.

    Planned spending is the amount the Dáil provided for the year, including any supplementary estimate agreed later in the year. What was spent is the outturn, audited by the Comptroller and Auditor General. Each department's account explains the reasons for large differences between the two.

    A department's budget is divided into programmes, and each programme into subheads, the lines on which money is provided and accounted for. Health and Social Protection each have a single programme, so their subheads are grouped here under the headings used in their accounts.

    A few subheads record a small refund rather than spending, and are shown with no size.
