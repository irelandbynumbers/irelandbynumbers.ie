About this data

Figures are for general government, which covers central government, local authorities and other public bodies, measured under the EU's ESA 2010 rules. They differ from the Exchequer figures the Department of Finance publishes each month, which cover central government only.

Taxes and other revenue go into a common pool rather than being set aside for particular services, so the chart shows totals in and out, not which tax pays for which service. The main exception is PRSI, which is paid into the Social Insurance Fund and pays for State pensions and other social insurance benefits. That fund is part of general government, so it is included in both sides.

In years when the State spends more than it raises, the gap is shown as borrowing. In years when it raises more than it spends, the difference is shown as a surplus. The surplus in 2024 includes a once-off payment of tax owed by Apple, following a ruling of the Court of Justice of the European Union. Borrowing in 2010 includes the cost of supporting the banks, which is counted under transport and the economy.

Spending by purpose follows the EU's classification of the functions of government (COFOG). Eurostat last revised this breakdown before the CSO's latest totals, so each area has been scaled by the same small amount so that the areas add up to the CSO's total spending for the year. The adjustment is 0.3% or less in every year shown.

Income tax includes the Universal Social Charge. Social protection covers pensions, jobseeker and disability payments, child benefit and housing supports. Government and debt interest covers running departments, collecting taxes, foreign affairs and interest on government debt. Transport and the economy covers roads and public transport, farming, energy, research and supports for business and employment. PRSI includes other social contributions, such as those paid by public servants towards their pensions.

Figures are in current prices, not adjusted for inflation.
